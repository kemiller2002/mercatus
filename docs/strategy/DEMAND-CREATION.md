# Echelon Foundry Demand Creation Strategy

**Mercatus status:** Proposed for review on feature branch; this does not replace existing canonical commercial domain requirements.

**Status:** Proposed for adoption | **Version:** 1.0 | **Date:** 2026-10-14
**Scope:** Echelon Foundry and every initiative marketed through the Sales & Marketing / Marketing OS capability.
**Implementation target:** Canonical sales-and-marketing repository, after confirming its current owner/path; Praxis-managed intake and work-item traceability.

## 1. Strategic thesis

> Reveal important problems people have learned to tolerate. Demonstrate their true consequences with evidence. Make credible improvement imaginable, measurable, and accessible. Earn the right to offer a solution.

We do not fabricate need, inflate losses, or create anxiety by exaggeration. We help people recognize consequential existing problems, understand their causes and options, and act when evidence justifies doing so.

**Primary operating model:** Research -> Recognition -> Consequence -> Possibility -> Proof -> Action -> Learning.

**Demand creation** addresses people who don't yet frame their situation as a purchasing problem. **Demand capture** serves people already researching remedies. We need both; creation is the primary differentiator, not the sole acquisition mechanism.

**North-star business result:** Incremental qualified commercial conversations and opportunities attributable to increased recognition of validated problems, ultimately tied to won revenue and profitability, not content volume.

## 2. Strategic principles and guardrails

1. Lead with the customer's experience, not our product category, architecture, AI, or feature list.
2. Distinguish observation, interpretation, hypothesis, causal explanation, and measured result. Preserve counterevidence.
3. Prioritize validated pain over clever positioning. Lack of buying authority, urgency, access, or feasible remedy can disqualify a market even where the problem is real.
4. Never convert sample scenarios, benchmark data, or industry averages into individualized ROI without appropriate qualification and inputs.
5. Explain mechanisms: what creates the loss, under what circumstances, which interventions work, and when the problem may not exist.
6. Produce genuinely useful educational material and diagnostics with or without a purchase; offer credible non-Echelon options where they fit.
7. Market by **problem / trigger / buyer**, then map relevant initiatives, not by isolated products competing for attention.
8. Support multiple stakeholders: practitioners (recognize), executives (economic impact), finance/procurement/security (justify and approve).
9. Require explicit human approval for public claims, prospect contact, social publishing, automation permissions, and consequential experiments.
10. Automate bounded research and drafting; do not build an autonomous content-factory or outreach-spam machine.
11. Preserve the Marketing OS existing model of MarketHypothesis, ProblemHypothesis, Evidence, ICP, Campaign, Asset, Result, explicit state transitions, approval rules, and learning.
12. Make decisions reversible: unsuccessful campaigns must inform repositioning, product decisions, or de-prioritization, not just trigger more posts.

## 3. The customer journey

| Stage | Prospect's question | Our value | Success evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recognition | "Is this happening to us?" | Scenario, anti-pattern, observable symptom, self-check | Measured change in problem recognition |
| Consequence | "Does it matter?" | Causal explanation, range-based impact estimate, qualitative risks | Prospect confirms relevance and priority |
| Possibility | "Can it be different?" | Alternatives, demonstration, counterfactual, trade-offs | Prospect identifies plausible path |
| Proof | "Can I trust this?" | Reproducible examples, transparent assumptions, references, limited-scope pilot | Qualified evaluation or diagnostic request |
| Action | "What's my next step?" | Low-risk diagnostic offer, consultation, workshop, implementation option | Meeting with appropriate authority, paid pilot, opportunity |
| Learning | "What did the market teach us?" | Structured evidence and controlled hypothesis transitions | Updated ICP, priority, messages, roadmap |

Offer an ungated self-assessment by default; request contact information only where necessary for a specific, consented follow-up or personalized delivery.

## 4. Portfolio problem maps (initial hypotheses, not established claims)

| Initiative | Problem to investigate | Recognition asset | Commercial path | Trigger to test |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **EDF** | Organizations repeatedly treat symptoms because systemic causes are never established | Engineering rework / cause-vs-symptom diagnostic | Paid discovery and diagnostic engagement | Missed roadmap, persistent incident patterns, new VP Eng |
| **Clarity** | Decisions and assumptions are lost, revisited, or inconsistently applied | Decision friction assessment with traceability exercise | Decision-system workshop or pilot | Cross-team rework, repeated architecture debates, AI workflow adoption |
| **HelixNote** | Technical evidence is plentiful but inconsistent or difficult to translate into useful explanations | Evidence-to-explanation demonstration with confidence and provenance | Limited-scope technical diagnostic | Slow incident diagnosis, inconsistent analysis, audit pressure |
| **Echelon Foundry** | Apparent engineering underperformance conceals structural delivery constraints | Delivery-system assessment, cost-of-rework walkthrough | Advisory engagement and targeted modernization | CTO transition, missed releases, investor due diligence |
| **Praxis + Ordo** | AI-assisted engineering raises apparent throughput without reliable completion evidence or coordination | "You said it's done. Prove it" evidence-chain demo, handoff rework analysis | Implementation assessment, licensing/services as appropriate | Growing use of agent coding, rising verification load |
| **Vitium / Dokimos** | Defect recurrence and quality signals are disconnected from the process decisions that caused them | Defect recurrence & prevention scorecard | Quality/defect workflow assessment | Regressions, slow incident closure |
| **Forma / Folio** | Inconsistent UI and print systems create accessibility and rework costs | UI consistency, accessibility, document-quality audit | Component adoption engagement | Multiple apps, design drift, compliance work |

Other initiatives join through the standard problem-map template after triage. Not every initiative needs a separate campaign. A single validated customer problem can introduce multiple complementary solutions without confusing the buyer.

Each map MUST record: customer observable symptom, specific causal hypothesis, affected segment, current workaround, cost category, evidence/counterevidence, buyer and hidden buyer, actual trigger, incumbent alternatives, differentiator, diagnostic, trustworthy proof, low-risk offer, desired action, guardrails, owner, and next review.

## 5. Priority and selection

Rate opportunities on six 0–3 dimensions using explicit evidence: **(a)** customer pain/severity, **(b)** economic impact, **(c)** reachable decision maker, **(d)** urgency/trigger frequency, **(e)** ability to demonstrate credibly, **(f)** delivery feasibility/fit. Track uncertainty separately, not as score zero. Record disqualifiers, counterevidence, and conflicts of interest. Weight scores only after agreeing an explicit priority model. Review with human oversight.

Do not launch a commercial campaign until there is a documented ICP hypothesis, problem hypothesis, customer-language evidence, plausible economic consequence, responsible buyer, credible offer, measurement plan, and counterevidence review. Lack of evidence means *research*, not *approved campaign*.

## 6. Research plan

For each initial problem cluster:

- Review internal diagnostics and engineering traces where consent permits, customer interviews, public engineering retrospectives, documented failures, competing approaches, job posts, and buyer discussions. Search for disconfirming patterns as well as support.
- Conduct **10–15 exploratory conversations per cluster as an initial learning target**, not a statistically representative validation sample. Include users, economic buyers and procurement/finance/security influencers where relevant. Avoid leading questions and avoid asking only whether they like the product.
- Ask for a recent incident, consequences, attempted remedies, evidence the problem is getting worse or better, what would cause an investment, how budgets work, and who could veto action.
- Distinguish awareness, severity, willingness to act, access to budget, and willingness to pay. Interview conclusions must retain the small-sample limitation.
- Conduct an explicit alternatives analysis including doing nothing, process improvement, existing tools, and a competitor. A problem's existence doesn't establish an Echelon advantage.

## 7. Content and channel strategy

Every validated problem gets an **evidence package**:

1. Recognizable scenario: named role, operating context, specific observable symptom.
2. Diagnostic: 3–10 minute useful exercise or instrument, with transparent scoring.
3. Mechanism: how the problem originates and propagates; when the model would be wrong.
4. Consequence model: ranges, inputs, and what cannot be estimated.
5. Improvement demo: comparison against baseline and alternatives, with a measured or clearly illustrative result.
6. Business brief: language for an executive and cross-functional buying committee.
7. Offer: no-pressure diagnostic, workshop, paid assessment, or proof-of-value with explicit scope.

Prioritize Echelon's first-party site, professional technical writing, talks, targeted outreach to consenting or appropriately sourced business contacts, and informed participation in relevant LinkedIn, Reddit, developer and business communities. Each third-party channel has its own rules, authorization gate and measurement. Treat comments as research signals and community participation, not opportunities for indiscriminate promotion. Support Facebook/X where an explicit audience hypothesis justifies them, rather than broadcasting by default.

Repurpose one research-backed canonical narrative across channels without fabricating facts or changing approved claims. Distinguish educational content, positioning, and solicitation; honor opt-out and platform policy.

## 8. Experiment design

Compare at least two approaches within a reachable segment where practical:

- **A: Problem-led** — distinctive scenario -> consequence -> diagnostic -> possible remedy.
- **B: Product/service-led comparator** — conventional capability/benefit description with comparable offer and distribution.

Pre-register audience, eligibility, allocation method, unit of randomization (when used), distribution budget, measurement windows, conversion definitions, decision threshold, and stopping rule. Avoid declaring statistical significance from tiny samples or biased traffic. When sample size is too small, report descriptive outcomes and uncertainty instead.

**Metrics hierarchy:**
- Exposure: relevant reach, attention, qualified visits.
- Recognition: baseline/post understanding or problem-specific self-identification; participant language matches.
- Relevance: reported severity, urgency, ability to act, recommended internal sharing.
- Commercial: diagnostic request, accepted meeting, buyer-qualified conversation, pilot, opportunity, proposal, win.
- Economics: acquisition effort/cost, cycle time, revenue, delivery margin, lifetime value where observable.

Event attribution may be multi-touch and imperfect. Preserve organic, direct and unknown sources. Never infer causal lift from raw before/after change without appropriate design. Do not count engagement as purchase intent.

## 9. Operating architecture

Extend existing Marketing OS objects and transitions rather than replace them:

- `ProblemMap`: ties symptoms, triggers and buyers to existing ProblemHypothesis and MarketHypothesis.
- `RecognitionJourney`: links the ordered assets/experiences and intended evidence at every step.
- `DiagnosticDefinition` and `DiagnosticRun`: validated scoring, version, user disclosures, consent, result and unknowns.
- `ImpactModel`: input provenance, units, formula version, sensitivity, uncertainty and scenarios.
- `EvidencePackage`: scenario, analysis, counterevidence, alternatives and approved claims.
- `Experiment`: preregistration, comparator, assignment, results and limitations.
- `BuyingGroup`: practitioner, owner, budget holder, procurement/security/finance stakeholders and tailored evidence.

These are proposed extensions. Reuse an existing canonical type or field when equivalent; create new types only to close a demonstrated gap. IDs and source links must trace from hypotheses through requirement -> work item -> artifact -> event -> evidence -> commercial learning.

Operational integrations should respect current Echelon patterns: Ordo for legal domain states/transitions, Praxis for work/evidence, Forma for interactive UI, Folio for print deliverables, Aegis for unexpected boundary failures, Limen for browser interaction when applicable, and pinned released dependencies. Fides/Arca are integration candidates, **not assumed operational prerequisites**. Start with locally reproducible/static-first experiments using GitHub Pages where feasible.

## 10. Governance and publication

Research, analysis and drafts may be automated. Publish/contact decisions require explicit authorization scoped by channel, audience, volume, content version and time period. Approved claims are immutable/versioned; material revisions require review. Prospect and diagnostic data are minimized, consented where necessary, access-controlled, retention-limited, redacted in telemetry, and not sent into LLM workflows without an approved policy. Offer deletion and opt-out paths where required. No covert scraping, impersonation, fake testimonials, invented benchmarks, dark patterns, or false urgency.

## 11. Launch plan (90 days, adjustable to evidence)

**Days 1–15: Foundation and selection.** Inventory initiatives and existing Marketing OS coverage; finalize schema extensions and evidence standards; select three problem maps; recruit initial interviewees; document baselines; establish analytics consent, approval and attribution definitions.

**Days 16–35: Discovery and assets.** Conduct interviews; record supporting/contradictory evidence; build one useful diagnostic and one evidence package per pilot; test diagnostic clarity, accuracy and accessibility; review messaging with both technical and executive stakeholders.

**Days 36–60: Controlled launch.** Ship EDF, Clarity and HelixNote pilot journeys with approval gates; distribute through two validated channels per campaign; run problem-led vs conventional-message comparisons; preserve events and objections.

**Days 61–90: Learn and scale.** Evaluate commercial evidence, revise hypotheses and portfolio priorities, stop unsupported campaigns, improve strongest journeys, plan further automation only for repeated successful workflows. Add capture-oriented pages/search content to convert already aware demand.

**Capacity guardrail:** Avoid shipping all channels and agents before validating the first three customer problems. Prefer one end-to-end working loop over a large collection of untested integrations.

## 12. Decisions and stopping criteria

- **Advance:** meaningful buyer-specific recognition, verified pain and urgency, credible differentiation, qualified next-step conversions.
- **Refine:** high recognition but low urgency; wrong buyer; unclear proof; insufficient diagnostic accuracy; weak channel fit.
- **Pause:** evidence too sparse, privacy/compliance questions, ambiguous measurement, misleading ROI or unverifiable claims.
- **Stop:** replicated disconfirming evidence, no economic importance or authority, poor fit, unacceptable acquisition economics, or better alternatives that invalidate the proposed offer.

No numeric conversion target is represented as an established benchmark. Set provisional thresholds only after initial baseline collection and record them before further experiments.

## 13. Research grounding and limits

- LinkedIn B2B Institute, *95-5 Rule*: https://business.linkedin.com/advertise/resources/b2b-institute/b2b-research/trends/95-5-rule — supports reaching future buyers, but 95/5 is a broad heuristic, not a verified segment-specific statistic.
- Ehrenberg-Bass Institute, *Category Entry Points in a B2B World*: https://ebims.emdev.au/category-entry-points-in-a-business-to-business-b2b-world/ — supports association with the buying situations that trigger recall.
- Edelman and LinkedIn, *2025 B2B Thought Leadership Impact Report*: https://www.edelman.com/index.php/expertise/Business-Marketing/2025-b2b-thought-leadership-report — supports educating wider buying groups, but survey findings do not establish causal efficacy for Echelon campaigns.

**Open assumption:** The central engineering rework/decision/evidence problems are commercially compelling for identifiable buyers. The pilot program is intended to test, and may disconfirm, that proposition.
